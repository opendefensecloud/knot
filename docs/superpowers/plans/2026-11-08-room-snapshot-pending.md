# Room snapshots and SyncStep2 without yrs's shifted pending encoding (implementation plan)

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** After this fix, no knot code serializes a page's live doc with yrs 0.27.3's `encode_state_as_update_v1`. Its `merge_pending_v1` writes pending blocks at shifted clocks once a doc's pending update holds overlapping Skips, so today's room snapshots, SyncStep2 replies and `ExportState` name blocks the room never had. Content that waits for a missing dependency must still survive a reboot, reach joining editors and drain when the dependency arrives, and boot replay must stay bounded.

**Architecture:** Every state encoding of a live doc goes through `knot_crdt::pending::encode_without_pending`. That is yrs's `encode_diff_v1`: the doc's own blocks and delete set, without `merge_pending_v1`. Pending content is never re-encoded from a doc that merged several updates. It travels as the original bytes of the log rows that parked it:
- **The room tracks those rows.** `pending::ParkedRows` holds the rows whose apply changed the doc's pending content, trimmed to an oldest-first cover of the pending structs and the pending deletes. Each own row is tied to its writer job by a token until its rev is known.
- **Snapshots name them.** Each snapshot stores the rows' revs in a new column, `doc_snapshots.pending_revs`. Before writing, the room replays the snapshot's bytes and those rows on a scratch doc, exactly as boot will, through the underflow guards. When that check fails and the pending content has a faithful encoding, the room persists that encoding as a new row and names it instead.
- **Boot re-applies them.** Boot applies the snapshot, then the rows after it, then those rows last, all through the same guards: the live doc held their content only pending while it applied the rows after the snapshot.
- **Joining editors get them.** A joining connection receives the rows' bytes as sync Update frames between SyncStep2 and SyncStep1. The socket writer sends this handshake itself, before any fan-out frame.

Snapshots written before the fix have `pending_revs IS NULL`. Boot does not use such a snapshot as stored when its bytes may be shifted (they name blocks past a gap and park content when applied), or when an older pod wrote it during the rollout. An older pod's snapshot is replaced by the newest trusted snapshot of the page. A legacy snapshot whose bytes may be shifted (they name blocks past a gap and park content when applied) is cut to its trusted prefix, and the page's whole log is replayed over it through the underflow guards. Every other legacy snapshot boots as before, from its bytes and the rows after it (Ruling 7). Legacy pending content that no log row carries is persisted once as a log row. The history builder's genesis, and every view of a baseline, starts from a baseline's trusted prefix when the baseline may be shifted. M1.3's builder already ran in 0.9.12 and 0.9.13 without these rules, so `BUILDER_VERSION` goes to 2 and every page's history is rebuilt once after the upgrade; that rebuild drops the gap checkpoints version 1 derived (keyed on the page's builder version and its genesis checkpoint's write time, never on a gap checkpoint's own `created_at`), and gap content is found again from the snapshots still kept (Ruling 14). The builder's dirty loop, not only its once-a-minute sweep, picks up pages below `BUILDER_VERSION`, so the rebuild runs at the first build's pace. Until a page's version-2 genesis, the released history list reports it as catching up, also when version 1 gave it up. The live room pre-checks every inbound, `ApplyUpdate` and catch-up update against the blocks it holds past a gap, as boot does, so a reboot that integrated stuck content cannot meet the reproduced underflow on the next reconnect (Ruling 25). The nesting check measures drained pending types from an encoding of every block of the clients the apply inserted for, so its cost follows bytes, never clock lengths (Ruling 12).

## Review Focus

1. **A page whose content stays pending for good, opened for weeks by editors who reconnect.** Re-sends of the parking update change the pending fingerprint in some shapes (overlapping Skips) without adding ids. Snapshots must keep being written, and `pending_revs` must keep naming the oldest rows that parked the content. After a restart whose boot integrated stuck content past a gap (Rulings 8 and 17 allow it), the reconnecting author's re-send (the GC block of its lost edit) must not corrupt the room (Ruling 25). Pinned by Task 3's `a_resend_of_the_pending_content_adds_no_row_to_the_cover` and Task 4's `content_parked_for_good_keeps_snapshots_and_its_parking_rows`, `a_resend_after_a_reboot_that_integrated_stuck_content_does_not_corrupt_the_room` and its legacy variant.
2. **The rolling upgrade, with 0.9.13 pods and fixed pods on one page.** A 0.9.13 room boots from a fixed snapshot (it ignores `pending_revs`) and then writes a NULL snapshot without the parked content. A later fixed boot must still re-park that content and drain it, without dropping text the trusted snapshot held, and 0.9.13 pods must keep loading, saving and snapshotting while the migration runs. The most common shape on the pages this fix targets is a 0.9.13 room open across the migration on a page whose pending update holds overlapping Skips: its pre-migration snapshot (the trusted one) and its flagged one both hold shifted bytes, and boot must start from the trusted row's prefix with no phantom and still drain. Pinned by Task 3's `a_snapshot_an_older_pod_wrote_after_the_migration_boots_from_the_last_trusted_one`, `a_flagged_snapshot_boots_from_the_newest_trusted_one_and_keeps_its_text`, `a_flagged_snapshot_over_a_shifted_pre_migration_snapshot_boots_from_its_prefix`, `a_shifted_flagged_snapshot_without_a_trusted_one_boots_from_its_prefix`, `ordered_log.rs::a_0_9_13_room_open_across_the_migration_on_a_shifted_page_boots_from_the_prefix_and_drains` and Task 2's `snapshot_pending_revs_migration_runs_under_live_0_9_13_traffic`.
3. **An editor opens a page that holds hundreds of parked rows, with the collab limits on (the default).** A lost update followed by the same client's typing parks every keystroke row, and the cover keeps each one (no compaction: Ruling 4). The join must not hang, the joining editor must not be dropped as a slow consumer while other editors type during its handshake (up to the writer's 8 MiB queue), and the handshake must not be throttled or cut by the liveness pings. What the joining yjs editor sends back after the handshake must name only content the room sent it. Not guaranteed, and unchanged by this fix: a writer whose client merges its pending content into its SyncStep1 answer (yjs and yrs both do) is closed by the server's 4 MiB inbound message cap when the page parks 4 MiB or more (Ruling 5's costs). Pinned by Task 4's `offline_sync.rs::a_join_with_300_parked_rows_syncs_and_drains` (limits off), `collab_rate_limit_integration.rs::a_join_with_300_parked_rows_is_not_throttled`, `a_join_with_3000_parked_rows_completes_in_bounded_time` and `a_joiner_whose_handshake_is_slow_keeps_its_place_while_others_type` (limits on), ws_writer's `room_frames_queued_during_the_handshake_are_buffered_not_dropped`, and `KnotProvider.test.ts`'s `answers the room's SyncStep1 with nothing it was not sent`.
4. **Legacy snapshots: a page from before 0.9.9 whose parking rows the old GC deleted, and an older pod's snapshot whose bytes hold shifted phantoms.** The first page's legacy snapshot holds the only copy of content that waits on another client's lost edit; it must survive the upgrade, and later snapshots must be written. Pinned by Task 3's `a_legacy_page_whose_parking_row_was_collected_keeps_its_pending_content`, `an_older_pods_snapshot_with_shifted_pending_content_boots_without_phantoms` (built on Task 1's detection and content properties) and Task 4's `carried_legacy_pending_content_is_named_by_the_next_snapshot`.

3. Ruling: parked content survives a reboot through `doc_snapshots.pending_revs`. Snapshots are not withheld merely because content is pending, and they are never stamped below the lowest pending rev.
   - Why:
     - Withholding stops snapshots for good on pages whose content stays pending forever (M1.2 found such rows), and their boot replay then grows without bound.
     - A lower stamp breaks the M1.2 invariant that the builder's gap probe relies on (a stamped snapshot at R equals `base ∪ replay(R)`): every row between the stamp and the real watermark would show up as authorless gap content.
     - The column mirrors M1.3's `doc_history_checkpoints.pending_revs`, which handles the same defect.
   - This overrides the M1.3 ruling sentence "snapshots withheld while the doc has pending content". Ruling 9's withholding is a safety net for a failed re-park check, and it re-parks to end itself.
   - Cost if wrong: one nullable column, and one extra row read per kept row at boot.
4. Ruling: `pending_revs` is an oldest-first cover of the doc's pending structs and pending deletes, kept in `ParkedRows`. Each own row is tied to its writer job by a token.
   - What is recorded: a row is recorded when its apply changed the pending fingerprint (`ApplyMeasured::pending_changed`, `RowApplied::pending_changed`) and the doc still holds pending content.
     - The row's claims are `PendingClaims { structs: insertions(true), deletes: delete_set() }` of its bytes. Its GC set (`insertions(true) − insertions(false)`) is cached with it for the underflow pre-check.
     - The doc's pending claims (`pending::claims`) are `structs = pending_update().update.insertions(true)` and `deletes = pending_ds()`. These are in-memory ids, which the encoding defect does not touch (`knot-history/tests/replay.rs:268-276` relies on this).
     - Structs and deletes stay separate everywhere. One id can be pending twice: as a parked struct, and as a parked delete of that struct (the generator's `delete_before_insert`, or an author whose earlier update was lost typing and then pressing backspace). A merged set would let a delete-only row cover a parked insertion, or the other way round.
   - The cover:
     - It is recomputed at every record and every snapshot (`ParkedRows::prune`).
     - Walking rows in rev order (unassigned rows last, in record order), a row is kept only when its structs meet a pending struct, or its deletes meet a pending delete, that no row kept before it covers in the same set. Rows marked by `ParkedRows::exclude` (Ruling 9) are never kept. Ids name content, so rows that cover every pending struct and every pending delete re-park the same content.
     - When nothing is pending, the rows are cleared.
   - Revs of own rows:
     - An own row is recorded with no rev, and `record` returns a `RowToken` (a per-room counter).
     - The room pushes one `Option<RowToken>` per job it hands to the writer (`None` for a job it did not record), and `on_applied` pops it and calls `ParkedRows::assign(token, rev)`. Assigning to a row that `prune` dropped is a no-op.
     - Why: the writer sends one `Applied` per job, in job order (`writer.rs:14-16`), but `prune` can drop a recorded row before its `Applied` arrives (a re-send that adds no ids, or a drain between hand-over and `Applied`). Pairing by position ("the oldest unassigned row") would then give a kept row another job's rev, and the snapshot would name a row that does not hold the parked content.
     - Snapshots need no extra rule: `may_snapshot` already requires `in_flight == 0` (`room.rs:1166-1171`), so every kept row has its rev.
   - Why the cover:
     - In some shapes a re-send changes the fingerprint without adding ids: re-applying an update behind overlapping Skips, and the shifted full-state re-sends of tabs opened before the fix. In most shapes a re-send changes nothing (`engine.rs:150-153`) and is not recorded at all. Without the cover, every fingerprint-changing re-send would add a row to `pending_revs` for as long as the content stays pending.
     - Oldest first prefers the small original edit rows over re-sends, which can carry GC blocks: the underflow shape.
   - Cost: `prune` runs a pass of set operations over the cached claims at each record, so a session that parks k rows costs O(k²) set operations, with no decode. That is about 45,000 for Review Focus 3's 300 rows.
   - Cost if wrong: a dropped row whose ids were not in fact covered. The re-park check withholds the snapshot instead (Ruling 9).
   - Ruling: k is unbounded while the content stays pending, and the cover is never compacted into one re-park row — in the keystroke shape k grows with the session that typed behind the lost update and then stops, so it is bounded by what one author typed before reconnecting (the author's SyncStep1 answer then carries the lost update and drains everything); compacting would exclude the original rows in favour of an author-less `carry_rows` row, which is sound only where property (d) shows `Faithful` content right for merged pending updates, and would take history credit from the author — cost if wrong: on a page parked for good after a long session, every join sends k frames (and yjs re-merges its pending update on each, O(k²) struct reads in the browser), every snapshot attempt replays k rows on a scratch doc in the room actor, and boot reads k named rows. Task 4's `a_join_with_3000_parked_rows_completes_in_bounded_time` and `pending_state.rs::a_cover_of_3000_keystroke_rows_stays_cheap` measure k = 3,000 and the report records the timings, debug and release; if either bound fails, stop and report for a ruling on compaction. The bound that matters to editors is the room actor's stall: `check_reparks` runs inline in the actor on every passing snapshot attempt (every 200 rows by default, plus the idle trigger), and while it runs the 256-slot event channel and the shims' `send(..).await` back up for every editor. So the gate is one check at k = 3,000 under 250 ms in a release build (`a_cover_of_3000_keystroke_rows_stays_cheap` asserts it when `!cfg!(debug_assertions)`, and Task 4 Step 4 runs it with `--release`); if it fails, stop and report for a ruling between compaction and running the check on a blocking task over a cloned state.
5. Ruling: a joining connection receives the kept rows' original bytes (`JoinState.pending`) as sync Update frames, after SyncStep2 and before SyncStep1. The socket writer sends this handshake before it forwards any frame from the room. A re-join (the shim's answer to a client SyncStep1) sends pending frames only to a connection the room had dropped. No "drained content" frame is fanned out.
   - How:
     - `ws_writer::run` gains `handshake: Vec<Vec<u8>>`, sent frame by frame under the same cancellation checks as every other frame, before it sends any frame from `out_rx` or its first ping. Both shims move their handshake frames there, and spawn the writer right after the Join reply (`knot-server/src/room.rs:77-145`, `board_room_shim.rs:60-111`).
     - While it sends the handshake, the writer keeps reading `out_rx` into a local queue, up to `HANDSHAKE_BUFFER_BYTES: usize = 8 * 1024 * 1024` of frame bytes and `HANDSHAKE_BUFFER_FRAMES: usize = 65_536` frames (presence frames are tiny and frequent, so bytes alone would let hundreds of thousands queue). It then sends the queue in order, and while the queue drains it keeps reading `out_rx` into the queue under the same bounds; the main loop starts only once the queue is empty. Each send (handshake frame or queued frame) is created once and pinned (`let send = sink.send(msg); tokio::pin!(send);`) and polled across `select!` iterations until it completes, so a frame is never re-created, sent twice or reordered when the `out_rx` branch wins. Why the drain keeps reading: draining up to 8 MiB on the same slow socket takes as long as the handshake did, and a busy page fills the 256-slot channel meanwhile. Why: the room registers the connection in `on_join`, before the handshake goes out, and `fan_out` drops a connection whose 256-slot channel is full (`room.rs:1146-1159`). From then on `on_inbound` drops everything that connection sends (`room.rs:1238`), while its socket stays open, and with the collab limits on the shim never re-joins it (`admit_sync_step1`, below). A handshake of many parked rows, or of one large one, on a slow socket would otherwise turn other editors' typing into a silently dead session. Ruling: buffer in the writer, bounded by bytes, rather than give the joiner a larger channel or exempt its next SyncStep1 from `admit_sync_step1` — the channel size is the room's slow-consumer rule for every connection, and the exemption would reopen the full-state encodes #91 bounds — cost if wrong: up to 8 MiB (or 65,536 frames) more memory per joining socket while its handshake and the queue's drain last; a joiner whose handshake and drain outlast that much of other editors' frames is still dropped as a slow consumer, as any socket that slow is today.
     - The doc shim's re-join sends `Event::Resync` instead of `Event::Join`. The room answers it like a join. `pending` holds `parked.frames()` only when `conn_id` was not in `conns` when the event arrived: `fan_out` (`room.rs:1146-1160`) drops a slow connection without telling the shim, and `on_join` re-registers it (`room.rs:1214-1230`). Rows fanned out while it was dropped did not reach it. The shim sends those frames after its SyncStep2 through `out_tx`. The writer is running by then, so the send waits but cannot block for good.
     - With the collab limits on (the default since #91), the shim answers only a connection's first client SyncStep1 (`ConnectionThrottle::admit_sync_step1`, `rate_limit/collab.rs:677`); later ones are ignored. So with limits on a connection gets at most one `Resync`, usually the one answering the SyncStep1 a client sends when it connects, which finds it registered and sends no pending frames. Ruling: keep main's rule and do not exempt a dropped connection's later SyncStep1 — the limit exists to bound full-state encodes in the room actor, and a dropped connection already lacks every fan-out frame since, not just the parked rows — cost if wrong: with limits on, a connection the room dropped after its first SyncStep1 never gets the parked rows over that socket and sees the drained content only after it reconnects, as it already misses every other edit until then.
     - Frames are never merged into one update with `yrs::merge_updates_v1`: that is the buggy path.
   - Why:
     - The joining client then holds the same pending content as the room, under the real ids. When the dependency later arrives through any path (another editor, `ApplyUpdate`, another replica's catch-up), the room fans out its raw bytes, as today. The client drains on its own.
     - A joining client that itself authored the missing dependency integrates the rows at once, and its SyncStep1 answer drains the room.
     - Today the shim sends its handshake into the 256-slot channel (`knot-server/src/room.rs:60`) before the writer exists. With one frame per kept row, a page with 255 or more parked rows would block the shim forever, and fewer rows plus fan-out could fill the channel so that the room drops the joiner as a slow consumer. Handing the handshake to the writer, which drains the channel into its bounded queue meanwhile, keeps the channel for fan-out only, as today.
     - What a joining yjs editor sends back: right after the parked frames the room sends SyncStep1, and yjs 13.6 answers with `encodeStateAsUpdate`, which merges its own pending structs and pending deletes into the reply (`mergeUpdatesV2` over `store.pendingStructs` and `store.pendingDs`, `yjs/src/utils/encoding.js`). The room persists that reply raw under the joiner's `by_user_id` when it changes the pending fingerprint, and fans it out. So yjs's re-encoding of merged pending content must be faithful, or every join on such a page writes phantoms into the log. Task 1 Step 5 checks it, Task 1 Step 6 stops for a ruling before Task 4 if yjs shifts, and Task 4's `answers the room's SyncStep1 with nothing it was not sent` pins it in CI.
     - Ruling: no Rust test of a yrs client's reply — knot's only collaborative client is the web editor (yjs); a third-party client built on yrs 0.27.3 would answer with yrs's shifted encoder, which is Ruling 16's case (the room cannot tell a re-sent phantom from an offline edit) — cost if wrong: such a client writes phantoms on pages with parked content, as it does before this fix.
     - A registered connection's re-join needs no frames: it got them at its join, and every row recorded since was fanned out to it (a parking apply persists and fans out its raw bytes).
     - This is the pre-fix behaviour without the shift. It needs no special encoding for content that drains past a gap.
   - Cost: on pages with pending content, the join payload grows by the cover's bytes, and a slow socket takes that much longer before its first fan-out frame, which the writer holds in its queue meanwhile.
   - Cost: a re-registered connection whose re-join frames fill its channel can be dropped again. It then catches up at its next re-join or reload. Before the fix, its re-join carried yrs's pending bytes.
   - Cost (predates this fix): a writer who joins a page holding 4 MiB or more of parked content is closed when it answers the room's SyncStep1. yjs (`encodeStateAsUpdate` over `pendingStructs`) and yrs 0.27.3 (`encode_state_as_update_v1` runs `merge_pending_v1` whatever the state vector, `transaction.rs:142-144`, `:375-391`) both merge their whole pending update into that answer, and the server caps inbound messages at `MAX_WS_MESSAGE_BYTES = 4 MiB` (`knot-server/src/lib.rs:27`, `:463-464`); the shim's read loop ends the session on the read error (`knot-server/src/room.rs:189-192`). Before the fix the same content reached the writer inside SyncStep2, with the same result. Viewers get no SyncStep1 and are unaffected. Task 4's slow-handshake test therefore answers SyncStep1 pending-free (it pins the writer's queue, not this cap).
   - Pings and the read half's silence limit (#91's `Liveness`: a ping every 30 s, silence 75 s) are unchanged. The writer starts its ping timer before the handshake as today (`ws_writer.rs:56-60`), so a ping that comes due during a long handshake goes out right after it (`MissedTickBehavior::Delay`). The read half hears the client's own SyncStep1 and presence meanwhile, so a slow handshake does not end the session. The collab budgets charge inbound frames only (`charge_inbound`), so the handshake costs the joiner no budget.
6. Ruling: rollout safety comes from `DocSnapshot.older_writer`, computed from `_sqlx_migrations.installed_on` of `20261114140000`, so no second column is needed. A flagged latest snapshot is replaced by the page's newest snapshot that is not flagged (`SnapshotStore::latest_trusted`).
   - Why `older_writer`: a 0.9.13 room that boots from a fixed snapshot misses its parked content, and its own snapshot (NULL, no pending content) then looks trusted. Only older pods write stamped NULL rows after the migration (fixed pods always write `Some`). Fixed pods start only after the Job, so no fixed snapshot exists before the migration commits, and an older pod's write that started earlier cannot have lost fixed content. Both timestamps are the database's `now()`, so clock skew does not enter. An upsert that promotes a legacy row also sets `created_at = now()`, so it is flagged too.
   - `installed_on` is `DEFAULT now()` of the migration's own transaction (sqlx-postgres 0.9.0 `migrate.rs:133`, `:239-241`), so it is the transaction's start, before `knot_lock_tables` gets its lock. Older pods' snapshots that commit between the helper's lock attempts are flagged too.
   - Why the trusted snapshot: a flagged row can lack only content parked below the snapshot the older room booted from. When that was a fixed snapshot, its `pending_revs` names the rows, and the log holds every row after its rev. A pre-migration NULL row is judged by Ruling 7. Booting from it reads only the rows after it unless its bytes may be shifted; then Ruling 7's legacy fallback replays the whole log from their trusted prefix, never from the flagged row's bytes. Either way it keeps text it held past a gap when its own bytes are not shifted, even when the flagged row's bytes may be.
     - `latest_trusted` orders like `latest` (`snapshot_rev DESC NULLS LAST, snapshot_seq DESC`) and skips flagged rows. That trusted snapshot then follows its own rule (Ruling 7 or the trusted path), and every fallback it takes starts from its bytes, never the flagged row's (Task 3, rule 2). Only when none survives does boot replay the whole log over the flagged row (rule 4). It replays over the flagged row's bytes, or over their trusted prefix when `pending::may_be_shifted` holds. A 0.9.13 room that held no pending content wrote unshifted bytes, so its text past a gap stays.
     - Ruling: this PR's `PgSnapshotStore::gc` keeps the `latest_trusted` row as a third keep set beside its 5 newest and one per day (Task 2), rather than leaving that to M1.4's `thin` — the hourly GC (`gc.rs:62`) runs on fixed pods from the upgrade on, and a page that 0.9.13 rooms snapshot 5 times after the last fixed snapshot would otherwise lose its trusted row to it — cost if wrong: one more kept row per page that older pods edited.
     - 0.9.13 pods run the same GC with 0.9.10's statement under the same advisory lock (`gc.rs:50-53`), so during a long rollout their GC can still delete the trusted row. Such a page then boots by rule 4 over the flagged row (pinned by Task 3's `a_page_whose_trusted_snapshot_an_older_gc_deleted_boots_from_the_whole_log`). The CHANGELOG and the Helm README say that a short rollout avoids it.
     - The boot counts `rollout` either way. `BootFallback.whole_log` tells the two apart.
   - A seq collision: a fixed room whose watermark is the flagged row's (`snapshot_seq` is the seq of the row at the watermark) targets the same `(doc_id, snapshot_seq)`. The `doc_snapshots_epoch` trigger skips the upsert of a stamped row (`RETURN NULL`), so the flagged row stays latest. `insert_stamped` therefore returns whether it wrote (`rows_affected() == 1`). A skipped write is not counted in `knot_room_snapshots_total`. It logs at debug, and the room resets its snapshot counters as for a write, since nothing newer exists at that watermark.
     - Ruling: the fixed write does not replace the flagged row. The trigger returns NULL for any update of a stamped row, whatever the upsert's `WHERE` says. Replacing it would take a DELETE of a stamped row on the snapshot write path, or a trigger change that 0.9.13 pods' writes would meet mid-rollout. Neither is needed for correctness: `rollout` boots are correct, only slower.
   - Cost if wrong: pages that older pods edited during the rollout read more rows at boot (every row after an older trusted snapshot, or the whole log). This lasts until a fixed pod writes a snapshot at a newer watermark, that is, until the page's next edit, since an idle page's fixed snapshot collides with the flagged row. This shows on `knot_room_boot_fallbacks_total{reason="rollout"}`.
   - Cost: a 0.9.13 room, or a 0.9.13 read-only load (the Markdown refresh), that boots from a fixed snapshot ignores `pending_revs`, so it never holds that pending content. When the content's dependency arrives at such a room, its live doc, Markdown cache, search entry and exports lack the drained text, and its joiners do not see it. A fixed pod's next boot of the page restores it (the trusted path re-parks it from the log), and the Markdown cache follows at the page's next refresh. The CHANGELOG and the Helm README say so, and that a short rollout avoids it.
7. Ruling: a legacy snapshot that is not flagged is untrusted when its bytes may be shifted (`pending::may_be_shifted(bytes)`: they name blocks past a gap and park content when applied). An untrusted snapshot boots from `pending::trusted_prefix(bytes)` (Task 1), then every row of the page (`RowsAfter::All`, the legacy path of `hydrate.rs:111-126`), with reason `legacy`:
   - Every other legacy snapshot that is not flagged boots as today, from its bytes: `Rev` rows for a stamped one, `All` rows for an unstamped one, every row through the guards (Ruling 8), even when its bytes name blocks past a gap or park content. When its bytes park content, boot takes `carry_rows` from the doc right after the bytes are applied, and Ruling 19 persists all of `carry_rows` when `derive` over the applied rows leaves pending ids uncovered (`carry_rows` encodes the base's whole pending content; it cannot encode a part). The room's cover then keeps a carried row only for ids no older row covers (`prune`, Ruling 4). No fallback is reported.
   - Every fallback (rule 4) uses the same test for its base: the prefix when `may_be_shifted` holds, the bytes otherwise.
   - Ruling: bytes that park content but are not `may_be_shifted` boot from their `Rev` rows, not the whole log (review round 4) — property (b) shows such bytes hold no phantom, and a stamped such row already equals `base ∪ replay(R)` with its pending content (the M1.2 invariant), so replaying rows at or below R adds nothing but `derive` finding the original parking rows, which Ruling 19's carry replaces; the replay would re-apply an underflow-shaped re-send at or below R that 0.9.13 never re-applied, and the guards would then make such a page `unlogged` at every open and fail its read-only loads (Ruling 8) — cost if wrong: one author-less carried row per such page instead of naming the original rows.
   - Why:
     - The shift needs pending content: `merge_pending_v1` changes nothing when the doc holds none (`transaction.rs:375`). So bytes whose fresh apply parks nothing were not shifted, and yrs integrates blocks past a gap whenever their origins are held. Such blocks are real text: a page that lost an update before M1.1 and was edited afterwards holds visible text past a gap. Distrusting them would drop it on every such page.
     - Shifted blocks sit past their client's first missing clock, because v1 writes clocks implicitly after each Skip. Task 1's property checks both claims on every generated state: every state with phantoms parks content when applied fresh, and names blocks past a gap.
     - Re-applying rows over a base that holds blocks past a gap can meet the skip underflow (`block_store.rs:318`). Every row boot applies therefore goes through `GuardedDoc` (Ruling 8), so unshifted bytes stay the base and keep their text past a gap. Only bytes that may be shifted are cut to the prefix.
     - The rows of pages from 0.9.9 on are all in the log, so the whole log restores what the prefix leaves out. M1.2 persists a parking apply raw.
     - Bytes that park content without being `may_be_shifted` must give a `Faithful` pending update when applied fresh, or Ruling 19's carry has no struct row for them. Task 1's property (e) checks that premise on every generated state and checks the carried content against a reference; Task 1 Step 6 names the fallback if it fails.
   - Cost: on a page whose rows the pre-0.9.9 GC deleted, an untrusted snapshot that may be shifted loses what only it held past the gap: parked content, text past the gap (from phantoms that editors integrated before the fix, or legitimate text), and prefix text whose origins lie there. It is not a guess: such content cannot be told from shifted phantoms. The warn line reports `dropped` (text below the gap whose origins lay past it, certainly lost) and `dropped_past_gap` (phantoms and text past the gap together, an upper bound). The CHANGELOG and the Helm README say that such pages can lose text, and that history views lose it too (Ruling 20).
   - Cost: an idle page with an untrusted snapshot replays its whole log at each open and each read-only load until it is next edited. The trigger keeps a stamped row immutable, and the exit snapshot reuses the same `snapshot_seq`.
   - Residual: phantoms that a room booted before the fix already integrated stay. Such a room took in shifted blocks whose origins it held, and its next snapshot holds them as ordinary blocks and parks nothing. This ruling trusts that snapshot, and nothing can tell those blocks from typed text. The text stays on the page, and the builder may report it as gap content (Ruling 13). The CHANGELOG and the Helm README say so.
   - Task 1 Step 6 names the fallback if either property claim fails.

Ruling: every row boot applies goes through one `pending::GuardedDoc` over the boot doc: the rows after a stamped snapshot (`RowsAfter::Rev`, rules 1 and 3), the `pending_revs` rows (rule 1), and every whole-log row (`RowsAfter::All`, rules 0, 3 and 4). The `pending_revs` rows also go through it at snapshot time, on a scratch doc.
   - Why the rows after a snapshot too: the snapshot's bytes can hold blocks past a gap (Ruling 7: real text past a lost edit), and a row after it can then be the reproduced `block_store.rs:318` underflow, a debug panic and a silent wrap in release. The pre-check costs nothing while `past_gap` is empty, and the post-check runs only for rows that name a client in `past_gap`.
   - Order (rule 1): the rows after the snapshot first, in rev order with today's contiguity loop, then the `pending_revs` rows, in rev order. The live doc held the kept rows' content only pending while it applied the rows after the snapshot. Applied first, a kept row can integrate stuck content past its client's gap (Rulings 11 and 17), and a later row with a GC block for that client then meets the underflow the live doc never met. Ruling: kept rows last, not first (review round 4) — with kept rows first, M1.3's reproduced shape (`t2_only` kept, the GC re-send `.1` after the snapshot) is refused at every boot, and rule 4's whole log in rev order meets the same order again, so the page boots `unlogged` at every open and never snapshots; `check_reparks` replays no row after the snapshot, so the check and a boot with nothing after the snapshot still apply the same order — cost if wrong: a row after the snapshot that needed a kept row's content integrated first (it named an id the kept row parks) parks until the kept row is applied, and the closing retry below integrates it.
   - The closing retry (review round 4): yrs 0.27.3 retries its pending update only when a missing client's `get_clock` (the end of its block list, `block_store.rs:437`) moves past the clock recorded in `missing` (`transaction.rs:838-842`). A kept row K that fills a Skip below its client's list end moves no clock. So with kept rows last, a row R' after the snapshot that names an id inside K's content parks, the dependency D after the snapshot arrives, K then integrates, and nothing retries R': the rebooted doc keeps R' pending although the live doc (where D's retry re-applied K and R' as one merged update) showed it. That breaks Ruling 17's relation 2 and serves a doc without text the room showed before the restart. So `GuardedDoc::retry_pending` runs once after the last row of every boot rule and after the kept rows in `check_reparks`: when the doc holds a pending update, it sets each `missing` entry's clock to 0 (`WriteTxn::store_mut().pending_update_mut()`, public in yrs 0.27.3) and applies an empty `Update`, so yrs re-applies its own pending update in memory, as D's retry did live (`transaction.rs:874-880`). It goes through the same pre-check (over the pending update) and post-check as a row. Ruling: force yrs's own retry rather than re-apply the rows whose claims meet the remaining pending content (review round 4's suggestion) — re-applying a row whose content stays stuck merges its remainder into the pending update again (an id-neutral re-send, which in the overlapping-Skips shape changes the pending fingerprint and can turn a `Faithful` pending update `Shifted`, Ruling 4), while yrs's retry takes the pending update out and re-parks only what still cannot integrate; Task 3's deterministic test pins it, and if yrs does not retry that way, the fallback is the re-apply pass (Task 3 Step 6) — cost if wrong: one apply of the pending update per boot and per check, bounded by the pending bytes.

9. Ruling: `Room::snapshot` withholds the snapshot when `prune` returns `Uncovered` or `check_reparks` fails. Before it withholds, it re-parks the content once when it can. The room keeps `withheld: Option<Withheld>` and `reparked: Option<PendingClaims>`.
   - `Withheld { missing: PendingClaims, reason: &'static str, claims: PendingClaims, revs: Vec<i64> }`. `check_reparks` returns `Refusal`, which is `Uncovered(PendingClaims)` or `Underflow(i64)`, but a withholding always ends on `Uncovered` (below), so `Withheld` keeps its `missing` set and the reason (`underflow` when the attempt excluded a row, else `uncovered`).
   - Exclude first: every `Underflow(rev)` refusal calls `parked.exclude(rev)`, records `last_excluded = Some(rev)`, then prunes and checks again, until the check passes or prunes to `Uncovered`. `check_reparks` replays only `parked.checked_rows()`, and `prune` never keeps an excluded rev, so a refused rev is never one already excluded (`debug_assert!`), and the loop runs at most once per kept row. So a second (or third) kept row that refuses is excluded on the same attempt, whether or not a re-park already ran. When the loop ends with a pass, the snapshot is written with no re-park. The `underflow` warn's `rev` is `last_excluded`.
   - Re-park: when the loop ends `Uncovered` (after exclusions, or because `prune` found the cover incomplete), the pending update has a faithful encoding (`pending::carry_rows(&doc)` is `Some`) and `reparked` differs from the current claims, the room persists `carry_rows` as new rows, as Ruling 19 does (reason `uncovered`, or `underflow` when an exclusion caused it). It records them with their tokens, fans each one out to the registered connections as a sync Update frame (`fan_out(&wrap_sync_update(row), None)`), and sets `reparked`. The carry rows cover every pending struct and delete by construction, and a faithful encoding comes from a single update with no held GC block. The next attempt, after their `Applied`, checks again. If the carry rows fail too, they are excluded like any other row and the snapshot stays withheld: `reparked` allows one re-park per set of pending ids.
   - Why the fan-out: an `uncovered` or `underflow` re-park writes content that no recorded row may carry, so editors who joined earlier may lack it and would not see it drain until they reconnect. Re-applying it is a no-op for those that hold it. The `legacy` carry (Ruling 19) runs before the event loop, when no connection is registered.
   - The final flush (`Room::run`'s `self.snapshot().await` after the event loop, `room.rs:683-691`) runs the same check and the same re-park, but fans nothing out: `snapshot(final_flush: bool)` passes the flag to `repark`, which skips the fan-out when it is set (the connections are closing). Ruling: keep the re-park at shutdown rather than only withhold — the writer flushes its jobs when the room ends, and the next boot's `derive` finds the row, so a page whose room never reached a snapshot attempt (a `missing_row` boot, an `uncovered` cover) is mended at its first shutdown instead of staying uncovered across sessions — cost if wrong: one author-less row per room exit on such a page, until a snapshot names it; `withheld` and `reparked` end with the room, so the next room may re-park once more if its own cover is again incomplete.
   - Comparisons: `withheld`, `reparked`, `Withheld.missing` and `Refusal::Uncovered` compare `PendingClaims` by set semantics (Global Constraints), so an empty client entry left by an `IdSet` diff never makes two equal claims differ, which would warn, count and re-park again on every attempt.
   - `Faithful` is judged by ids (Ruling 12), and a re-park row replaces the original in the cover. So Task 1's property (d) checks a `Faithful` encoding's content against a reference, on live docs that merged several pending-changing applies. If (d) finds a mismatch, Task 1 Step 6's fallback restricts this re-park to pending content from a single pending-changing apply.
   - Each attempt: when `withheld` holds the current claims and revs, the room skips the check. Otherwise it prunes and checks. A withholding equal to `withheld` is skipped quietly. A different one warns (Global Constraints text for its reason), counts once with the reason, and is stored. A passing check clears `withheld` and writes the snapshot. Every withheld attempt sets `updates_since_snapshot = 0`, so the next attempt comes after `every_n` more rows, not on every row (`snap_state.updates_since_snapshot`, counted in `on_applied`, `room.rs:1015-1040`).
   - Boot derives the rows from everything it applied (`ParkedRows::derive`), and pending content that the snapshot's own bytes held and no applied row carries gets a carried row (Ruling 19), whichever rule used those bytes as base. The known paths that still leave pending content uncovered are a `missing_row` boot (Ruling 8) and a base that is a trusted prefix (its pending part is dropped); re-parking ends the first, and the second holds nothing pending from the bytes. Re-parking also ends a withholding the underflow check causes, unless the content's encoding is `Shifted`.
   - Cost: one author-less row per re-park, which the history builder credits to nobody: the original rows already parked the same ids, so replaying the re-park row parks nothing new.
   - Cost if wrong: a page whose pending encoding is `Shifted` and whose rows fail the check stops snapshotting while that content stays pending, which shows on the counter.

A refused row follows rule 4's rule (skipped when `GuardedDoc::holds`, else a failed row), and the boot then reports `underflow` with `whole_log`. Why guard it: the log can itself hold the corrupting order (M1.3 reproduced a room applying a re-send over a doc holding blocks past a gap), and unguarded it panics in a debug build and wraps in release.
  1. `pending_revs: Some(revs)` (trusted): `GuardedDoc::new` over a doc that applied the bytes. First the `RowsAfter::Rev(snapshot_rev)` rows through it, with today's contiguity loop: a refused one (`Refused::Underflow` or `Corrupt`) falls back with `underflow`; one that fails to apply is passed as today. Then `rows_at(revs)`, in rev order, through the same `GuardedDoc` (Ruling 8's order: kept rows last): `Refused::Underflow` or `Corrupt` falls back with `underflow`. A rev the log lacks, and a row that is `Refused::Apply`, is skipped: boot keeps the rows after the snapshot and the remaining named rows and reports `missing_row` with `whole_log == false` (Ruling 8). `store.reads()` is therefore `[After(Rev(snapshot_rev)), At(revs)]`.
  2. `older_writer` set: take `latest_trusted()`.
     - `Some(t)`: boot from `t` by rule 1 (`t.pending_revs` is `Some`) or rule 3 (it is `None`), with three changes. The boot reports one reason, `rollout`, whatever `t`'s own rule would report, and counts once. `carry` follows the `Boot::carry` rule, independent of the reason: it is set whenever the base is a legacy or flagged row's own bytes (rule 3, or rule 4 over bytes `may_be_shifted` does not flag) and `derive` leaves pending ids uncovered, and never from a prefix base. Every fallback from `t` (rule 1's or rule 3's `underflow`, rule 3's `legacy` fallback for bytes that may be shifted) runs rule 4 from `t`'s bytes or their prefix, never the flagged row's: only `t`'s writer held the rows the flagged row lacks; rule 1's `missing_row` stays on `t` (Ruling 8). `whole_log` is as that rule left it.
     - `None`: rule 4 from the flagged row's bytes, reason `rollout`.
  3. `pending_revs: None`, not flagged: if `pending::may_be_shifted(bytes)`, fall back with `legacy` (rule 4 from the trusted prefix; Ruling 7). Otherwise apply the bytes; when `pending::claims` is `Some`, take `carry_rows` from this doc before any row (Ruling 19). Then boot as today: `Rev` rows for a stamped snapshot, through the guard as in rule 1's rows after the snapshot, or `All` rows for an unstamped one, through the guard as in rule 4. A refused `Rev` row falls back with `underflow`; a refused `All` row follows rule 4's rule, and the boot reports `underflow` with `whole_log`. Without a refusal it reports no fallback, even when it carries.
  4. Fallback: discard the doc. Start a new one from `trusted_prefix(bytes)` when `pending::may_be_shifted(bytes)` holds, else from the bytes. Bytes that park nothing were not shifted, so their text past a gap stays (Ruling 7). Read `RowsAfter::All` (with the existing read-only fallback). When the snapshot the fallback started from is stamped at R, apply every row with rev ≤ R without the contiguity stop (a missing rev there is skipped), then set `through_rev = R` and `through_seq` = that snapshot's `snapshot_seq`, and run today's room-mode contiguity loop over the rows above R (Ruling 8: otherwise a missing rev at or below R would hide every later row). When it is unstamped, keep the legacy path's `base_rev = 0` and the loop over every row. Every row goes through one `GuardedDoc` over the base. A refused row is skipped and counted in `refused` when `GuardedDoc::holds` is true for it; otherwise it is passed as a failed row (room `unlogged`, read-only `BootError::Apply`). When the base is the bytes themselves, take `carry_rows` from it before any row (Ruling 19). Compute `dropped` and `dropped_past_gap` (Global Constraints): `insertions(true)` of the bytes, minus the booted doc's held ids and pending structs, split by the bytes' `Update::state_vector()` into below and `at_or_above`. `whole_log` is true.

  Every rule ends, after its last row, with one `GuardedDoc::retry_pending()` (Ruling 8's closing retry). `Refused::Underflow` there comes from the pre-check, before any apply, so the doc is unchanged and keeps the content pending: boot goes on without the retry. `Refused::Corrupt` is handled as a refused row of that rule (rule 1 and rule 3's `Rev` rows fall back with `underflow`; on a whole-log path the room is `unlogged` and a read-only boot returns `BootError::Apply`). In `check_reparks`, `Underflow` likewise skips the retry and `Corrupt` is `Refusal::Underflow` of the last row's rev.

  Every boot that leaves rule 1 or rule 3's "as today" branch, skips a named row (`missing_row`), or refuses a row, warns (Global Constraints: the snapshot text for `legacy` and `rollout`, the fallback-path text for `missing_row` and `underflow`) and counts `knot_room_boot_fallbacks_total` once with its reason, as it returns `Ok`. A boot that has a reason and refused a row reports the refusal only in `refused` (or as a failed row), not as a second count. A boot that returns `Err` warns and counts nothing for its fallback. Room and read-only boots share every rule. Only room mode fills `parked` and `carry`.
